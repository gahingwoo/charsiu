Ships were the first machines that people trusted with their lives at a distance
from land, and the arithmetic of that trust was worked out slowly, in losses. A
master could estimate latitude from noon and had no way at all to fix longitude
until a clock existed that would keep time on a moving deck through heat and cold.
The prize offered for such a clock was larger than the salary of the astronomer
royal for a working lifetime, and the man who eventually won it was a carpenter
who had taught himself the trade of clocks from a borrowed book on mechanics.
